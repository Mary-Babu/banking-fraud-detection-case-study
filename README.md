## Overview

Global bank fraud and scam losses reached **$579.4B in 2025**, and U.S. account-takeover losses grew **23% year over year** — even as banks keep investing in detection tools. This case study treats that as a real business problem, the way a Business Analyst would on the job: a sourced problem statement, root-cause analysis, SWOT, a proposed data-driven solution, and a full requirements-to-roadmap workbook, built entirely from public industry data (PwC, Federal Reserve, FTC, Nasdaq Verafin).

It's a practice exercise, not a live engagement — every assumption, constraint, and benchmark is labeled as such throughout.

## What's Inside

The deck follows a consulting-style narrative rather than a flat slide list:

- **Business Problem** — sourced stats on fraud and account-takeover losses, plus the growth-rate evidence behind them
- **Root Cause Analysis & SWOT** — a "5 Whys" chain landing on a data-accessibility problem, not an algorithm gap
- **Business Objective & Proposed Solution** — a BA approach: define the question, consolidate a minimum dataset, baseline, build a prioritization layer, frame impact honestly
- **Solution Design** — scope, stakeholders (goals & pain points), functional/non-functional requirements, a process flow with a feedback loop, a data dictionary, assumptions, constraints, and a risk register
- **Dashboard & KPIs** — a prioritization-dashboard mockup and six measurable fraud-ops KPIs
- **Future Roadmap** — where this would go with ML, real-time streaming, and modern data infrastructure, clearly scoped as out of bounds for this exercise
- **Skills Demonstrated & Final Thoughts** — the BA and data-analytics toolkit actually used, and the one idea the case study is built around

## Key Findings

- Global bank fraud & scam losses: **$579.4B** in 2025 (Nasdaq Verafin)
- Fraud scams are growing **19.3%/year**, more than 2x faster than unauthorized-transaction fraud (8.2%/year)
- U.S. account-takeover losses: **$15.6B** in 2024, up **23% YoY** from $12.7B (Federal Reserve / FinCEN)
- **53%** of Canadian financial-services CEOs say their top AI/analytics tool can't access all their organization's data (PwC) — the root cause this case study builds around

## Proposed Solution

Join login, transaction, and complaint data into one fraud-signal table using SQL, then surface it through a Power BI dashboard that risk-scores and ranks accounts — so investigators work the highest-risk cases first instead of a flat, chronological queue.
